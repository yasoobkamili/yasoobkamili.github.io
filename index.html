Nuss Gali

10 May 2026
We drove for ~140 km to Mahu Village on the Khari side of Banihal. We reached there at around 9 am after a 3.5 hour drive. We started our trek at 9:30 am after our local guide talked to security forces about permits and formalities. We crossed the first bridge and started walking through local houses and shops for about 25 minutes. Then we merged onto the trail and, after 1.5 km of gentle ascent through patches of ploughed agricultural fields, we reached Nunkoth, where a few dhoks are located beside a stream. We continued along the stream for another 1.5 km, gradually ascending, until we crossed it and reached the army camp at Mori at 10:45 a.m, where our identities were verified. On our left was snow capped Achhan Mal, one of the prominent peaks of the valley with an elevation of ~4190m asl. After a long break, we descended to the left of Mori then ascended back a small ridge and then walked through an open meadowy part until we ascended to a vantage point overlooking Ratan, a lush green valley with multiple dhoks and grazing grounds. Before the road connectivity to Mahu was established, Ratan used to serve as the traditional route for locals traveling across the region. We had our lunch on the way besides a stream just 1km before Nuss Gali. After lunch we gained about 225 metres of elevation and reached Nuss Gali at around 1:45pm. Nuss Gali connects Banihal's Mahu Valley to Kulgam's Gulzarabad. From the top we could clearly see prominent peaks like Huen Heng Peak (~4200m asl) of Kulgam, Didam (~4215m asl), Sunder Top (~3847m asl). On a clear day, you can even see the distant Kishtwar Himalayas, the peaks around Pahalgam, and possibly Harmukh(i'm not sure about it). I got to see many peaks of Kishtwar and Anantnag, one of them I suspect to be of Brahmma 2. After an hour at nuss gali, we started descending back to Mahu village and then left for Srinagar at 6:30 pm.

Trek Grade: Easy
Total Distance: 17 kms

Mawarnag

14 September 2025
We drove from Srinagar to Ahlan Gadol village and began our trek from there. The trail initially passed through a mildly dense forest, following prominent water pipelines for a few kilometers. After covering about 1.6 km, we reached Kassi. Due to recent flash floods, several bridges were damaged, forcing us to cross a few small water bodies on foot. Continuing for another 2.1 km, we reached Guddarwuin, a settlement area with a few dhoks used by the Gujjar community. This place had some well-placed wooden benches, making it a good resting point. From Guddarwuin, we turned right and ascended a steep section through a lightly forested mountainside for around 2 km, eventually reaching an open meadow(not that meadowy) known as Ali Brari. With fewer trees and over 20 dhoks scattered across the area, it is one of the largest clusters of dhoks I’ve seen so far. Ali Brari also serves as a good campsite, and we took a short break there. From Ali Brari, we followed a zero gradient trail, followed by a long descent, crossed a water body, and then began ascending a mildly steep ridge toward Inderwal. After resting there, we continued our ascent above the treeline and climbed the final ridge leading to Mawarnag 1, situated at an elevation of approximately 3790 m ASL. We reached the first lake at 1:00 pm. We then ascended a steep, rocky ridge to Mawarnag 2, reaching the second lake at 1:30 pm, located at approximately 3855 m ASL. We had lunch there until light rain began. At 2:00 pm, we started our descent, returning first to Mawarnag 1 and then continuing back towards Ahlan. I and four others were the first to reach the vehicles at around 6:00 pm. However, local security forces stopped us for trekking without prior permission. As many of group members were still on the trail, three of us went back to bring some of them down safely. By then, it was completely dark. I left my backpack in the car, put on my headlamp, and hiked approximately 3 km back from Ahlan to Guddarwuin through a dense forest in pitch darkness. We eventually met some of the remaining trekkers and guided them down. The security forces held us there until the entire group had assembled, after which we were allowed to depart from Ahlan at around 9:30 p.m.

Trek Grade: Moderate
Total Distance: 22 kms (ps: 28 for me though)

Trusar & Magroosar

27, 28 July 2024
We drove from Srinagar to Doodhpathri and started our trek from the Shaliganga river crossing. We retraced the trail up to Diskhal, which we had visited during the Ashtaar trek. At Diskhal, we enjoyed a noon chai break with nomads and then descended to the left. After crossing a rocky patch and a small ridge, we reached Bod Aangun . We traversed Bod Aangun via smooth trails and ascended to a ridge that serves as a vantage point to Naav and leads to the Chaskinar Campsite. We descended the ridge and walked straight to the campsite, where we pitched our tents and had lunch and rest. Some of us ascended the ridge between Trusar and Chaskinar, reaching the top where we saw the three lakes of the Trusar cluster and enjoyed the sunset. We descended back to our campsite in the dark, had dinner(PS: Butane can was not feeling good that night), and witnessed an amazing galaxy and constellations that night. After a few hours of sleep, we started walking at 5:30 am toward Magroosar Lake. I compared the actual southern view of Tatakooti Peak with a picture from Dr. Ernest F. Neve's book "Beyond the Pir Panjal - 1912" (there wasn't much difference). After crossing several ridges, we reached Magroosar Lake around 10 am. On our return, we encountered dense fog and an hour of rainfall. We packed up our campsite and descended back to Doodhpathri before driving back to Srinagar.

Trek Grade: Moderate
Total Distance Covered: 51 kms
Total Time: 35h:08m

Ashtaar Valley

02 June 2024
We began our trip with a 55-kilometer drive from Srinagar to Doodpathri, Budgam. The trail started from the Shaliganga river crossing at Doodpathri, with an initial steep ascent through a forest ridge leading to Diskhal Meadows. From this point, we caught a glimpse of Tatakooti Peak and enjoyed a noon chai (made with buffalo milk) break with nomads. After descending into the meadow to the right of Diskhal, we reached a water stream emerging from the Tatakooti glacier, which we crossed via a wooden bridge. We then ascended to a meadow and walked straight to Ashtaar Valley for lunch. On our return, we picked up some rotis and another cup of noon chai from the same nomads at Diskhal before descending back to Doodpathri and returning to Srinagar.

Trek Grade: Easy-Moderate
Total Distance: 23 kms




Panzkoot Waterfall

05 May 2024
The Panzkoot trek began with a ~70 km drive from Srinagar to Halan village in Manzgam, Kulgam, with necessary permits obtained from the Nihama Army Camp. The trail started from Halan with an initial steep ascent through a forest ridge, leading to a vantage point over the Upper Chiranbal Meadows.Some prominant peaks of shopian and kulgam are visible from these meadows, like Brahmasakli and more. After descending into the meadow which connected to several other trails such as Hakwas, Zajmarg, Indersar, Astanmarg, and Chirsar, we passed through a dense forest section to reach the Panzkoot Waterfall by 12:30 PM, where we had our lunch. During the return, we had a tea-coffee mix and interacted with local foragers collecting Guchi Mushrooms(Morel Mushrooms) . We got to see some Gucchi before descending back to Halan and returning to Srinagar.

Trek Grade: Easy
Total Distance: 12 kms


Sheeshnag

15 October 2023
The Sheeshnag trek began after a 90 km drive from Srinagar to Chandanwari (~2740 m ASL), though the journey was delayed due to two flat tyres, resolved with help from a local Tavera driver who provided a spare. After tea and pakoras in Pahalgam, we began the trek from Chandanwari. The trail, fully concretized by BRO as part of the Amarnath Yatra route, was easy to follow and allowed for a fast pace. Two of us quickly ascended to Pissu Top, rejoining another trekking group that started the trek one and a half hours before us. The trail then passed Zojibal, home to an Army camp and BRO quarters, where mild snowfall began. We reached Sheeshnag Lake (~3600 m ASL) around 12:30 PM after covering 9 km. Snowfall intensified, reducing visibility to 100–200 feet. The rest of the group arrived at 1:40 PM. After lunch, the sky cleared, revealing Sheeshnag Lake surrounded by fresh snow-covered peaks. We began descending at 3 PM and reached Chandanwari before departing back to Srinagar.

Trek Grade: Easy
Total Distance: 18 kms
Total Time on Trail: 7 hrs

Margan Lakes: Choharnag & Khellannag

03 September 2023
The Choharnag trek began with a 120 km drive from Srinagar to Margan Top, after which the trail started towards the left of the pass. A steep ridge was crossed within the first hour, and after approximately 4 km of trekking, we reached Choharnag 1, part of a cluster of four alpine lakes collectively named Choharnag (meaning "four springs"). Known as one of the easiest alpine lake treks in the region, the route continued to Choharnag 2, the largest of the four, followed by Choharnag 3, which I consider the most scenic amoung the four. Then, Choharnag 4, was reached before ascending a steep 200-meter ridge to Khellan Top (~4300 m ASL), a vantage point offering expansive views of Khellan Nag Lake, whose deep blue color was attributed to mineral content and sunlight angle. From this high point, Shilsar 1 and 2 were also visible through binoculars, previously visited on a trek. Lakes Choharnag 1, 2, and 3 were clearly visible from the ridge. After lunch at Khellan Top, we descended back to Margan Top and concluded the day with tea at Majid’s dhaba, also visited during the Shilsar trek.

Trek Grade: Easy to Moderate(for Choharnag) & Moderate to Difficult (for Khellannag)
Total Distance: 18 kms

The Arshan Valley

20 August 2023
The trek to Arshan Lake began after a ~110 km drive from Srinagar to Sinthan Top (few kilometers before Sinthan Top), a high-altitude pass in South Kashmir. From there, the trail led into Arshan Valley, and after a few hours of trekking, we reached Arshan Sar (also known as Drinyan Sar). The route then continued via a steep, rocky ascent to Drinyansar Pass (~4170 m ASL), which offers panoramic views of Zednag, Nehnag, and the Famber Valley. A sharp descent from the pass led to Zednag followed by Nehnag, a distinctive kidney-shaped alpine lake. I further explored the region by ascending to Hoksar 1 (Abdullah Sar on some maps) before descending back to Zednag. We then retraced the trail back over Drinyansar Pass, closely sighting a Himalayan Marmot and reached Sinthan Top by sunset, completing a high-altitude circuit through a rugged and scenic section of Sinthan.

Trek Grade: Moderate to Difficult
Total Distance: 14 kms

Shilsar, My First Alpine Lake

30 July 2023
The Shilsar Lakes trek began from Margan Top (~3700 m ASL), a high-altitude pass connecting Islamabad with Kishtwar, after a 120 km drive from Srinagar. The trail starts to the right of the pass with an initial steep ridge followed by a gradual ascent through alpine terrain. Out of the four Shilsar lakes, we visited only two on this route as the remaining are more accessible from a different approach. We reached Shilsar 1 (~3900 m ASL) at around 12:30 PM with an estimated surface area of 6310 sq. meters (season-dependent), followed by Shilsar 2 (~3920 m ASL) after a 20-minute trek where the we had lunch during rain and hail. The lakes marked my first alpine lake experience, with distant views of Nun and Kun peaks visible from Margan Top. I was the first to descent at around 4:00 PM, and had a cup of tea at a local dhaba owned by Majid, and departed from Margan Top at around 5:30 PM.

Trek Grade: Easy to Moderate
Total Distance: 11 kms

Mahadev Peak Basecamp: Lidwas

25 June 2023

Route Overview

Originally intended as a one-day summit attempt to Mahadev Peak (3966 m ASL), the plan had to be modified due to road closures in Srinagar caused by a cycle race, resulting in a two-hour delay. We initiated the trek from Scholar’s School, Dhara (2030 m ASL) without any breaks. After the initial stretch, the trail passed Padshah Pal, followed by an approach toward Bobjan, a section known for high bear activity and considered one of the more dangerous segments of the route. Several members reported past bear encounters here. Following multiple stream crossings, the trail entered Wovur Gul (I'm not sure about the name), a steep and energy-intensive climb. After gaining elevation in Wovur Gul, Lidwas became visible at a distance. It took about an hour from this point to reach Lidwas.

Lidwas & Above

Lidwas serves as a seasonal settlement for nomadic communities during the six months of summer. We reached Lidwas around 1:30 PM. Out of 20 members, 5(Faizan, Basit, Aakash, Sharik and Yasoob(me)) decided to push further above Lidwas for better vantage points of Mahadev Peak. We ascended an additional hour, reaching an altitude of approximately 3520 m ASL, which provided unobstructed views of Mahadev Peak and surrounding ridgelines. On the descent back to Lidwas, a wildlife sighting occurred, an animal with a furry tail was initially suspected to be a fox, but was later confirmed via binoculars to be a Himalayan Marmot, a first-time sighting for us.

Return Journey

While descending, we retraced the same route. At Bobjan, a group member narrowly avoided stepping on a green snake, marking another notable wildlife encounter. After exiting Bobjan, the trail gradually eased, allowing us to recover from the more challenging sections.

Trek Grade: Moderate to Difficult
Total Distance: 19.6 kms

Rainuer, The Endless Meadow

11 June 2023
The trek begins from Hirpora village in Shopian at approx 2385 meters above sea level. Trekking in this area usually requires prior permission due to its proximity to sensitive zones and the Hirpora Wildlife Sanctuary. At around 600 meters into the trail, we reached Zrado, followed by a gradual ascent to Baliwal. The trail transitions from forested sections of Shahkoot to more open terrain, and at approximately 6.5 km from the trailhead,we reached the Tsurgul Pass. This pass offers the view to Badgam's Shein Meuin Peak and first major alpine vista, opening up into expansive meadows and panoramic views of the Pir Panjal range, including prominent peaks like Brahmasakli(of Kulgam), Dhakyar(of Shopian), Sunder Top (of Banihal) and more. After crossing the pass, a further 3.5 km leads to the final destination, Rainuer Meadows at around 3185 m ASL. The meadows are vast, lush, and relatively less explored, believed to be the second-largest alpine meadow in Kashmir. The terrain is grassy, interspersed with boulders and occasional seasonal streams. Although the standard route is a circuit trek (Hirpora → Rainuer → Dubjan Bridge → Hirpora), we had to return via the same route due to an injury to one member. During the descent, we were warmly hosted by local nomads at a hut near the trail. Around 15 of us were served noon chai, roti, and fresh homemade butter. Despite offering to pay, the nomads politely refused any compensation, showcasing typical high-altitude hospitality(PS: Nomad's of Diskhal are an exception ).
Trek Grade: Easy to Moderate
Total Distance: 19 kms

Dumail, Naranaag

11 July 2021
The trek begins from Naranag village, situated at around 2277 meters above sea level. A few hundred meters into the trail, we pass by ancient Hindu temples, offering a quick historical stop. After approximately 4 km and 2 hours of trekking, Dumail is reached at ~2400 m ASL. The trail up to Dumail features minimal elevation gain and is rated as easy. The name “Dumail” is derived from “Du” (two) and “Mail” (join), marking the confluence of two rivers—Marchoi Nala and another stream originating from Gangbal Lake. These form the Wangath River. A distinctive wooden bridge at Dumail marks the crossing point to the next section of the trail. From Dumail, the trail transitions into a moderate gradient ascent through alpine meadows for the next 5 km. This section involves a steady climb with scenic views of the surrounding forested ridgelines and open pastures. After approximately 4 additional hours of trekking, the Harmukh peaks come into view, signaling proximity to Marchoi Valley, which sits at an altitude of about 2850 m.
Trek Grade: Easy to Moderate
Total Distance: 19 kms
